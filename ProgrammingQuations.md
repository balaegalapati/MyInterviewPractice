Key Problem Patterns

    Sliding Window: Used for problems involving subarrays or substrings, such as finding maximum/minimum sums, longest substring with unique characters, or a window meeting a condition. It optimizes brute force by reducing redundant calculations through dynamic window resizing.

    Two Pointers: Common in sorted arrays or linked lists, two pointers move through the data at different speeds or directions to find pairs, triplets, or to reverse/partition data. Examples include finding a pair with a given sum or merging sorted lists.

    Backtracking: Used for combinatorial problems like permutations, combinations, subsets, or solving puzzles (e.g., Sudoku). It explores all possible options recursively but prunes paths that don’t lead to solutions.

    Greedy Algorithms: These make locally optimal choices at each step aiming for a global optimum. Used in interval scheduling, coin change (with certain constraints), or selecting activities.

    Divide and Conquer: Breaks the problem into smaller, more manageable subproblems, solves them independently, and combines the results. Examples include merge sort and quicksort.

    Dynamic Programming: Solves complex problems by breaking them into overlapping subproblems and storing solutions to subproblems to avoid redundant work. Common in optimization problems like knapsack, longest common subsequence.

    Graph Traversal: Techniques like Depth-First Search (DFS) and Breadth-First Search (BFS) explore nodes and edges, useful for connectivity, shortest path, cycle detection problems.
